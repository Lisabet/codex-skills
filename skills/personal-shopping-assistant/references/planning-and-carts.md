# Planning and carts

Read this for a new shopping cycle, product selection, store comparison, or browser-based cart work.

## Start from the need

1. Read `stock.md`, `preferences.md`, the active part of `current-shopping.md`, relevant product notes, and relevant store records. Open old purchases only for a concrete question or duplicate check.
2. Turn `always_check` entries into a draft of needs, then apply known stock and current exceptions. Ask each `ask_each_order` question once per shopping cycle, preserve the answer in the current context, and do not repeat it.
3. Connect quantity to horizon, purpose, likelihood of use, and storage or freezing options. Treat cooking effort as a real constraint alongside price and ingredients. Do not require an exact meal plan unless it changes the choice.
4. Show a short draft: need, quantity or range, what is already available, what is skipped, and any remaining decision. Never invent a budget.
5. Once the list is agreed, continue all authorized cart work without seeking confirmation for every routine substep.

Keep four sections in `current-shopping.md`: `Buy`, `Skip this shop`, `In carts`, and `Needs decision`. A product may be split by purpose or store. Do not call matching categories duplicates without checking total quantity and purpose.

## Urgent now and cheaper later

When some needs must arrive before others or stores differ materially in price, availability, or delivery time, assign each need one urgency level: `now`, `by_tomorrow`, `can_wait`, or `reserve`. Derive urgency from the person's direct words, an agreed near-term use, or an objective deadline. Do not call an item urgent merely because it is out of stock. Ask only when uncertainty changes the scenario choice.

Always consider three scenarios:

1. buy everything now;
2. wait and buy everything from the cheaper store or delivery window;
3. buy the urgent part now and the rest later.

If a scenario violates timing, availability, an order minimum, or another hard condition, do not omit it. Calculate its cost from available data, mark it infeasible, and state why.

For every scenario, use the same required quantities and check availability, the nearest real delivery window, item totals, promotions, taxes where relevant, delivery, packaging and service fees, the order minimum, and the gap to that minimum. For a split scenario, include both deliveries and the inconvenience of receiving twice. Never present two unequal carts as a store price comparison.

If the urgent part is below the order minimum, compare three responses:

1. move useful products from the already agreed list into the urgent order;
2. place a below-minimum order with a separate fee only when the store explicitly permits it;
3. wait only when the urgency level allows it.

An ordinary delivery fee does not waive a hard item minimum. Do not add random filler that costs more than the fee it avoids. Any useful top-up must come from current needs or an explicitly confirmed strategic reserve.

Recommend the feasible scenario that meets timing and quality criteria at the lowest full cost. Show what arrives now, what waits, each order total, the minimum or remaining gap, both delivery costs, and savings against the next reasonable option. Preserve the split by quantity and purpose so the later cart is reduced correctly after the first order.

## Product selection

Check mandatory properties first, then compare suitable options on a common basis: weight, volume, item, length, sheet, or useful serving. Package count does not replace quantity. A larger package with a lower unit price is not economical when the excess will not be used.

Consider:

- ingredients, type, weight or volume, quality, and convenience;
- comparable unit price and the full cost of the needed quantity;
- shelf life and storage conditions without inventing an unknown expiration date;
- promotions, coupons, loyalty prices, delivery, packaging, service fees, taxes where relevant, and order minimums;
- allowed substitutions and store- or season-specific rules.

“Cheapest” means cheapest among the verified suitable options. Explain a material premium for quality or convenience. If it exceeds the agreed boundary, present one concrete decision to the user.

## Multiple stores

Compare equal quantities of comparable products. A difference between unequal carts is not a store premium. Show unavailable items, quality or convenience differences, delivery timing, and the full cost of every scenario separately.

After placing an order in one store, recalculate the unmet need in every other cart. Remove a duplicate only when purpose and agreed total quantity also match. Do not erase an intentional reserve as an accidental duplicate.

## Browser work and final check

Before changing a cart, read its live state and preserve the user's manual edits. After each add or removal, wait for the interface to update and verify the product card or cart. A planned action is not a completed action.

At the first timeout, stalled load, or suspicious state, perform at most one read-only check and report the symptom immediately. Never repeat a click, reload, or form submission blindly. After a disconnected browser session, reacquire the live tab and first determine whether the previous action took effect. Stop at an anti-bot challenge; never bypass it.

Before handing over a cart, verify the list, quantities, manual edits, unavailable and perishable items, promotions, delivery, fees, and full total. Press the final order or payment button only after a separate explicit instruction at that moment; authorization is separate for each store.
